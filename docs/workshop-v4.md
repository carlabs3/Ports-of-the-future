1. The workshop at a glance

Ports of the Future puts port stakeholders in the position of an adaptation team. Each table receives a port facing a specific mix of climate hazards, a limited budget and a deck of possible measures. The teams read the port’s vulnerability analysis, invest, and then face the extreme weather events drawn by a weighted wheel. Three rounds let them adjust their strategy and show the difference between a good strategy and a lucky one.

The workshop opens with an individual self-assessment, the Port Adaptation Readiness Index (PARI), which gives the room a picture of its own adaptation culture and feeds into the game as a corrector factor.

Learning objectives

- Recognise how a port’s geography, climate zone and history translate into a specific risk profile.

- Prioritise adaptation measures under a budget constraint and justify the trade-offs.

- Understand that protection is probabilistic: a well-prepared port can still be hit by an unlikely event.

- See how governance and organisational culture make technical measures more or less effective.

- Compare adaptation readiness across port types in the room and discuss what explains the differences.

Workshop flow

| **Time** | **Step** | **What happens** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 min | 1 · Introduction | Context: extreme weather and ports, SAFARI, rules of the session |
| 10 min | 2 · PARI | Each participant answers 10 questions on their phone (about 3 minutes). The screen shows anonymised results, averages by area and filters by port size, governance model, climate zone and coastal setting. |
| 5 min | 3 · Table culture | Each table averages its PARI. The average sets the table’s culture level and multiplier. |
| 10 min | 4 · Port challenge | Tables receive their port card, read it aloud, place their port on the map and review the seven events. |
| 20 min | 5 · Round 1 strategy | Tables choose cards with 6 coins and write a one-line rationale. |
| 10 min | 6 · Round 1 wheel | The app spins each port’s wheel (weights revealed now) and computes protection and damage. |
| 15 min | 7 · Round 2 | +2 coins, optional swap of one card, spin again. |
| 15 min | 8 · Round 3 | +2 coins, optional swap, final spin. |
| 10 min | 9 · Results | Luck score (damage across 3 spins) vs Strategy score (expected protection vs the port’s best possible). |
| 15 min | 10 · Debrief | What made a strategy good, what culture changed, what the room takes home. |

2. Step 1 · Port Adaptation Readiness Index (PARI)

Ten questions, two per area, four answers each, scored 0–10. Five areas × 20 points give an index out of 100. ‘I don’t know’ always scores low: not knowing your own budget or plan status is a readiness signal in itself. The structure follows the themes of the UNCTAD port industry survey on climate adaptation (plans, assessments, finance, measures) arranged as a maturity ladder.

Facilitator screen and messages to look for

The app shows the distribution of individual scores, a radar chart of the five area averages, the overall average and range, and the same views filtered by each profiling question. The patterns below are hypotheses to check against the room’s data, not results to announce in advance.

- Budget & Finance is often the weakest area: many ports recognise the risk before they fund it.

- Small ports may score lower on Organisation & People (no dedicated staff) while scoring similarly on Preparedness, where operational know-how compensates.

- Landlord and private ports may differ on Implemented Measures because equipment resilience sits with terminal operators, not the authority.

- Climate zone may shape which area is strongest: ports in storm-exposed zones often have tested emergency plans, ports facing slow-onset heat or drought less so.

- A gap between Governance (plans on paper) and Preparedness (tested drills) is a frequent and useful discussion point.

Step 2 · From individual PARI to the table’s culture level

Each table averages its members’ PARI. The band sets a multiplier applied to every protection score the table buys. Governance & institutional protocols and Capacity building & training are enabler cards: each one moves the table up one level (maximum Proactive).

| **Table PARI** | **Culture level** | **Multiplier** | **What it means in the game** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–25 | Reactive | × 0.8 | Measures deliver 20% less than on paper |
| 26–50 | Aware | × 0.9 | Measures deliver 10% less |
| 51–75 | Developing | × 1.0 | Measures deliver as designed |
| 76–100 | Proactive | × 1.1 | Measures deliver 10% more (capped at 10) |

**Why a multiplier: **institutional fragmentation and untrained staff reduce the effect of technically sound measures, a point made in the SAFARI governance report (D5.1). The size of the effect (±10–20%) is a game convention, not a measured value.

4. The seven extreme weather events

Storms, floods, wind and heat are the hazards ports report most often in the UNCTAD global survey; drought and wildfire are rising risks for southern European ports documented in SAFARI D3.10; snow and ice appear in SAFARI’s risk taxonomy (D3.13) and in the Dunkirk degradation model (D3.4). Fog, hail, lightning and tsunami were left out: they are either not climate-driven extremes or not addressed by any adaptation card.

| **Code** | **Event** | **Description** | **Parts of the port hit** | **Losses** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SS | Storm surge & coastal flooding | Low pressure and strong onshore winds push the sea above its normal level. Water overtops quays and dikes and floods low-lying terminal areas. | Quays, container yards, electrical substations, warehouses, ground-level equipment | ●●●● (4) |
| RF | Heavy rain & river flooding | Intense downpours overwhelm the drainage network, or prolonged rain raises the river above its banks. | Drainage, rail yards, low-lying storage, access roads, basements, lock gates | ●●●● (4) |
| HW | Heatwave | Several days of abnormally high temperatures, often with high humidity and warm nights. | Outdoor workers, crane and vehicle cooling, reefers, IT, power demand, rail tracks | ●●● (3) |
| WS | Windstorm & cyclone | Sustained high winds and violent gusts, from winter storms, tropical cyclones or local winds. | Cranes, moored vessels, container stacks, roofs, overhead lines, loose cargo | ●●●● (4) |
| DR | Drought & water scarcity | A long rainfall deficit lowers river levels and strains the water supply the port depends on. | Navigation channels, locks, barge traffic, draught limits, water-dependent operations | ●●● (3) |
| WF | Wildfire | Fire in the vegetation around the port or along its access corridors. | Access roads and rail, power lines, workers (smoke), fuel and chemical storage perimeter | ●●● (3) |
| SI | Snow, ice & cold snap | Heavy snowfall, freezing rain or days of deep frost that paralyse surface operations. | Cranes (ice), rail switches, road access, pipes and hydraulics, worker safety | ●●● (3) |

Sea-level rise and erosion are chronic stressors, not wheel events: they raise the baseline exposure of coastal ports over decades.

Card texts

**1. Coastal defence structures****   ●●●●● 5 coins**

Seawalls, breakwaters and revetments form the port's outer shield. They absorb and deflect wave energy before it reaches quays and yards, and stop most storm-surge flooding up to the storm they were designed for. A bigger storm can still overtop them. Expensive, long-lasting, and of no use against hazards that come from the land.

*Protection: SS 8 · WS 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal, Coralia*

**2. Elevated critical infrastructure****   ●●●● 4 coins**

Substations, control rooms, pumps and IT are raised above projected flood levels, and new berths are built higher. When water arrives, the port's nervous system stays dry. Very effective against coastal flooding and helpful in river or rain floods, but it only protects what has been raised.

*Protection: SS 7 · RF 4   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Coralia, Sabkha*

**3. Deployable flood barriers****   ●●● 3 coins**

Temporary barriers, including modular 3D-printed units, set up across gates, doors and low points when a flood is forecast. Much cheaper than a permanent wall and useful against both sea and river water. They only work if the warning comes early enough and the team knows where to put them.

*Protection: SS 5 · RF 5   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Riu Verd, Coralia, Sabkha*

**4. Green infrastructure ****&**** permeable surfaces****   ●● 2 coins**

Green roofs, rain gardens and permeable paving let rain soak into the ground instead of rushing into the drains. They cut flooding from heavy rain up to the storm they were sized for, and planted areas take a few degrees off the hottest afternoons. A true cloudburst will still overwhelm them.

*Protection: RF 5 · HW 2   ·   Ports: Meridia*

**5. Nature-based coastal ****&**** riverbank protection****   ●● 2 coins**

Restored wetlands, mangroves, salt marsh and planted riverbanks, sometimes built with dredged sediment, stabilise the shoreline and slow incoming water. They reduce erosion, soften surges and river floods, and bring biodiversity back. They protect less than concrete and take years to mature, but they grow stronger over time instead of weaker.

*Protection: SS 4 · RF 3 · WS 2   ·   Ports: Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**6. Structural health monitoring****   ●●● 3 coins**

Sensors on locks, quay walls, cranes and rail track measure stress, vibration and deformation around the clock. Monitoring stops no storm, but it shows where a structure is close to failing, so it can be repaired before a flood, gale, heatwave or hard freeze finishes the job.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 2 · WS 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal*

**7. Digital twin ****&**** predictive maintenance****   ●●●● 4 coins**

A virtual replica of a critical asset, such as a lock or a rail network, fed with live data. It predicts how the asset degrades under heat, frost, floods or low water and schedules maintenance before failure. Especially useful in river ports, where it helps run the lock when water levels turn critical.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 2 · WS 2 · DR 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Riu Verd*

**8. Early warning ****&**** forecasting system****   ●● 2 coins**

Port-specific weather alerts with operational thresholds for wind, rain, heat, frost and water levels. A warning stops nothing on its own, but a few hours of notice let the port secure cargo, stop cranes, move equipment and protect people. It helps a little against every hazard.

*Protection: SS 2 · RF 2 · HW 2 · WS 2 · DR 2 · WF 2 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**9. Real-time operations platform****   ●●● 3 coins**

A shared digital platform that reroutes vessels, trains and trucks before and during a disruption, anticipates congestion and organises the recovery. It doesn't protect assets: it keeps cargo moving while the weather does its worst, cutting the operational losses of floods, storms, low water and ice.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · WS 3 · DR 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Riu Verd*

**10. Emergency response plan ****&**** drills****   ●● 2 coins**

A plan that assigns roles, evacuation routes, communication chains and equipment-protection steps for each hazard, tested in regular drills. It protects people first and assets second, and reduces losses in almost every type of event. An untested plan is paper; a drilled one saves time, cargo and lives.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 3 · WS 3 · WF 2 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**11. Worker heat-protection protocol****   ● 1 coin**

Shaded rest areas, water stations, earlier shifts and heat-stress monitoring for outdoor staff. In ports, much of the cost of a heatwave comes from sick workers and lost hours, and this cheap measure tackles exactly that. It also helps when wildfire smoke makes outdoor work unsafe.

*Protection: HW 7 · WF 2   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd, Sabkha*

**12. Climate-resilient materials ****&**** retrofits****   ●●●● 4 coins**

Buildings, warehouses and workshops are reinforced with materials designed for high winds, driving rain, extreme heat and freeze-thaw cycles. Moderate protection against almost everything, strongest against wind, but it covers buildings, not yards, channels or cranes. Broad and solid, at a high price.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 3 · WS 5 · WF 2 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Meridia, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**13. Wind-resistant mooring ****&**** crane reinforcement****   ●●● 3 coins**

Reinforced mooring systems, crane tie-downs, storm pins, wind-speed interlocks and secured container stacks. Cranes and moored ships are where wind does its worst damage in a port, and this measure goes straight at that. Very effective in gales and cyclones, a little help when ice loads equipment, useless against water.

*Protection: WS 7 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal, Coralia*

**14. Backup power ****&**** microgrid****   ●●● 3 coins**

Generators, batteries and on-site solar keep pumps, cooling, reefers, communications and IT running when the grid fails. Power cuts make every disaster worse. Backup power matters most in heatwaves, when demand peaks, and in ice storms, when lines come down. It does little once water is inside the building.

*Protection: SS 1 · RF 1 · HW 4 · WS 2 · WF 1 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**15. Drainage system upgrade****   ●●● 3 coins**

Bigger stormwater pipes, new pumping stations and cleared channels, sized for today's downpours rather than those of 1980. The most direct answer to flooding from intense rain: water leaves the port as fast as it falls. A storm beyond the new design can still overwhelm it, and it does nothing against the sea.

*Protection: RF 7   ·   Ports: Meridia, Coralia*

**16. Alternative water supply ****&**** reuse****   ●●● 3 coins**

Rainwater harvesting, treated wastewater reuse and a second, independent supply such as a small desalination unit. When drought restricts the main supply, the port keeps washing, cooling, suppressing dust and fighting fires. Essential where water is scarce, and it adds a little cooling capacity during heatwaves.

*Protection: HW 1 · DR 6 · WF 2   ·   Ports: Meridia, Sabkha*

**17. Multimodal shift platform****   ●● 2 coins**

Rail sidings, barge connections and agreements that let cargo switch between road, rail and water when one route fails: flooded roads, closed terminals, low rivers, icy highways. It protects flows rather than assets, keeping trade moving around the damage.

*Protection: SS 2 · RF 3 · WS 3 · DR 2 · WF 2 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Riu Verd, Boreal*

**18. Artificial reefs ****&**** wave dissipation****   ●●● 3 coins**

Submerged structures, including 3D-printed reefs made from natural materials, break wave energy offshore before it reaches the port's defences, while creating marine habitat. They take the force out of storm waves and slow erosion, but a surge still raises the water level: reefs blunt the blow, they don't block it.

*Protection: SS 4 · WS 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Coralia*

**19. Governance ****&**** institutional protocols****   ● 1 coin**

Formal rules on who decides what, when and with whom, before and during extreme weather, shared by the port authority, terminal operators, city and emergency services. On its own it protects nothing. But fragmented governance quietly ruins good technical measures, and clear protocols make every other card work better.

*Protection: Enabler: +1 culture level   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**20. Capacity building ****&**** training programme****   ● 1 coin**

Regular, hazard-specific training for staff: e-learning modules, scenario exercises, cross-department simulations. Trained people improvise less and act faster. Like governance, its value lies in making the plan, the warnings and the equipment actually work under pressure.

*Protection: Enabler: +1 culture level   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**21. Adaptive dredging ****&**** sediment management****   ●●● 3 coins**

Drone and sonar surveys show where the channel silts up; dredging is targeted and the sediment reused to strengthen banks. For river and estuary ports it is the first defence against low-water navigation limits, and a clean channel also carries floodwater away faster.

*Protection: RF 3 · DR 6   ·   Ports: Riu Verd*

**22. Stormwater retention ****&**** storage basins****   ●●● 3 coins**

Basins and tanks catch peak rainfall before it floods the port, then hold that water for dry periods: dust suppression, cooling, firefighting reserves. One infrastructure for two opposite problems, too much water and too little.

*Protection: RF 6 · DR 3 · WF 1   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd*

**23. Parametric climate insurance****   ● 1 coin**

Insurance that pays out automatically when a measured trigger is crossed (wind speed, water level, temperature), without a long loss assessment. It prevents nothing physically, but it turns a financial shock into a manageable one and pays for a fast recovery. Transferring risk is not the same as reducing it.

*Protection: SS 1 · RF 1 · HW 1 · WS 1 · DR 1 · WF 1 · SI 1   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**24. Winterisation package****   ●● 2 coins**

De-icing systems for cranes and gantries, heated rail switches, snow-clearing equipment, insulated pipes and cold-weather gear for staff. During an ice storm it makes the difference between a paralysed port and a working one. Entirely specific: it does nothing the rest of the year.

*Protection: SI 7   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal*

**25. Firebreaks ****&**** vegetation management****   ●● 2 coins**

Cleared buffer strips, managed vegetation along the perimeter and access corridors, and joint plans with regional fire services. Wildfire reaches a port through its surroundings: access roads, power lines, the edge of fuel storage. Managing that interface keeps the flames, and most of the disruption, at a distance.

*Protection: HW 1 · WF 6   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd*

**26. Cool surfaces ****&**** shaded yards****   ●● 2 coins**

Reflective coatings on yards and roofs, shade structures over work areas and reefer stacks, light-coloured paving. Surface temperatures drop by several degrees, equipment overheats less and outdoor work stays possible for longer. A targeted answer to heat, with nothing to offer against water or wind.

*Protection: HW 5   ·   Ports: Meridia, Sabkha*

6. Scoring logic

Protection against the event drawn

When the wheel draws an event, the app takes every card the team owns that scores on that event, applies the culture multiplier c, and combines them as independent layers of protection:

**Protection P = 10 × [ 1 − (1 − ****c·s****₁/10) × (1 − ****c·s****₂/10) × ****… ]**

Each extra layer stops a share of what got past the previous one, so P never exceeds 10, two medium measures are worth less than their sum (two cards of 5 give 7.5, not 10), and one well-targeted measure beats several weak ones. Participants never do the arithmetic; the app does.

What the team sees after each spin

| **Result** | **Formula** |
| --- | --- |
| Losses avoided | P × 10 % |
| Damage bill (coins) | Event losses × (1 − P/10), rounded to 0.5 |
| Expected protection | Σ (wheel weight of each event × P for that event), in %: how well the whole portfolio covers the port’s risk profile, whatever the wheel drew |
| Strategy score | Expected protection ÷ best expected protection achievable for that port, budget and starting culture level (the app finds it by testing every affordable combination) |

Worked examples

**Boreal, table PARI 42 (Aware, × 0.9). **The team buys Winterisation (2), Wind-resistant mooring & crane reinforcement (3) and Governance protocols (1). Governance lifts it to Developing (× 1.0). The wheel draws Snow, ice & cold snap: P = 10 × [1 − (1 − 0.7) × (1 − 0.2)] = 7.6, so 76% of losses avoided and a damage bill of 0.7 coin instead of 3. Had the wheel drawn storm surge, P = 0 and the bill would be the full 4 coins. Expected protection: 51%. Best achievable for Boreal at 6 coins starting from Aware: 58%. Strategy score: 88%.

**Nord-Maris, same culture level. **The team spends 5 coins on Coastal defence structures and 1 on Governance. Storm surge drawn: P = 8, an excellent spin. But expected protection is 41% against a best of 51% (strategy score 80%): nothing covers the 3-in-10 chance of a windstorm beyond the seawall’s small effect, and an ice storm would cost the full bill. A team that bought the emergency plan, insurance and structural monitoring instead would score 51% expected protection with the same 6 coins.

Rounds

- Round 1: 6 coins. Cards are chosen before the wheel weights are known; the team relies on the vulnerability analysis.

- At the first spin the app shows the port’s wheel, which works as the port’s climate risk assessment.

- Rounds 2 and 3: +2 coins each. Unspent coins carry over. A team may return one card per round and recover half its cost (rounded down): infrastructure is hard to undo, and this rule makes the cost of an early mistake visible.

- Final ranking: the average Strategy score of the three rounds, which rewards teams who read the port well from the start. The damage bills of the three spins are shown alongside as the ‘luck’ result.

Why three spins do not remove luck, and how the scoring handles it

A simulation of the game (20,000 runs per port) compared the best 6-coin strategy with a median one. With a single spin, a median strategy matched or beat the best one 20–30% of the time in four of the six ports. Over three spins this fell to 0–17%, depending on the port. Three rounds reduce bad luck but do not remove it: at Nord-Maris, for example, the chance of drawing at least one 10%-probability event in three spins is about two in three.

That is the reason for the two scores. The damage bill tells the story of what happened; the Strategy score measures what the team controlled. Showing both, side by side, turns luck into the debrief message instead of an unfair ranking.

8. Debrief

- Which clue in your vulnerability analysis drove your first choice? Which one did you miss?

- Did your round 1 strategy survive the wheel? What did you change once you saw your port’s risk profile?

- Who had good luck and a weak strategy, and who had the reverse? What does that mean for how ports report on adaptation?

- Which cards did nobody buy, and why? (Look at insurance, biodiversity-friendly measures, monitoring.)

- How did your culture level change your results? What would raise it in your own organisation?

- Compare with the PARI screen: does the room look more like Reactive Coralia or Developing Sabkha?

Take-home messages

- Read the risk before buying the solution: the same card can be excellent in one port and nearly useless in another.

- No-regret measures (warning, plans, training) come first; heavy infrastructure follows when the budget and evidence allow.

- Being well prepared lowers the odds of a bad outcome; it does not remove them.

- Governance and skills multiply the value of every technical measure.

9. How the numbers were built, and their limits

The protection scores follow a rubric rather than individual guesses. Engineered measures designed against one hazard score 7–8, never 9–10, because design standards are exceeded by extreme events (SAFARI D3.10 documents dike overtopping at Dunkirk in 1953 and 2013). Physical measures that cover part of the hazard or part of the port score 4–6. Anticipatory and operational measures score 2–3. Early warning is anchored to the estimate that 24 hours of warning can reduce damage by about 30%, scored 2 here because ports can act on only part of a warning. Enablers have no direct protection and work through the culture multiplier.

The economy was then tested by computing every affordable combination for every port. A first version let four cheap ‘soft’ cards (warning, plan, governance, training) win in all six ports, which would have made the port cards irrelevant. Soft measures were therefore lowered, enablers turned into multipliers and insurance reduced. In version 4 the best 6-coin strategy differs from port to port and includes at least one card from the port’s own specific deck in all six ports; the best achievable expected protection ranges from 51% to 58%, so no port starts at a structural disadvantage.

**Limits to state in the debrief. **The scores are teaching estimates within an evidence-informed rubric, not published effectiveness coefficients; the literature does not provide clean percentages per measure and hazard. Costs are relative, not euros. Ports and data are fictional. Cyclone intensity in the Coralia chart follows the IPCC finding that the proportion of intense tropical cyclones has likely increased, not a claim that storms are more frequent.
