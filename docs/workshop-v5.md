# 1. The workshop

## The story

It is 2026. Each table is the adaptation and resilience team of a port. The board has asked an external consultancy for a climate vulnerability assessment, and the report has just arrived. The team has a first budget of 3 coins. It has to read the report, discuss it, agree on what matters most and decide which measures to put in place.

The game then moves forward in time. In 2031 and again in 2036 the team gets more money, can adjust its plan, and faces the weather of that year. The climate does not stand still between rounds: some events become more likely, and some hit harder.

## Learning objectives

- See how a port's location, climate and history shape its risks.

- Separate how likely an event is from how much damage it would do.

- Choose measures under a budget limit and explain the choice.

- Understand that a well-prepared port can still be hit by an unlikely event.

- See how governance and skills change what technical measures deliver.

# 2. The CAP index

The themes follow the UNCTAD port industry survey on climate adaptation: plans, risk assessments, finance and measures. 'I don't know' always scores low, because not knowing your own budget or plan is a readiness gap in itself.

## What the CAP index is used for

First, the room's anonymous results go on screen: average score, strongest and weakest areas, and differences by port size, governance model and climate zone. Things worth checking with the data: whether budget is the weakest area, whether small ports score lower on organisation, and whether plans exist on paper without being tested.

Second, each table's average becomes its culture level in the game. The culture level changes how much protection every card delivers:

| **Table CAP** | **Culture level** | **Multiplier** | **Effect** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0–25 | Reactive | × 0.8 | Cards deliver 20% less than printed |
| 26–50 | Aware | × 0.9 | Cards deliver 10% less |
| 51–75 | Developing | × 1.0 | Cards deliver as printed |
| 76–100 | Proactive | × 1.1 | Cards deliver 10% more (maximum 10) |

The Governance protocols and Training programme cards protect nothing directly. Each one moves the table up one culture level. The idea that weak governance undermines technical measures comes from the adaptation literature, including SAFARI's governance report (D5.1). The size of the effect is a game choice, not a measured value.

## Port 1 · Nord-Maris

**CLIMATE VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT · 2026 · prepared for the Port of Nord-Maris**

| **Location** | North Sea coast, Europe | **Climate zone** | Temperate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coastal setting** | Artificial / engineered coastline | **Port type** | Container & ro-ro hub |
| **Size** | Large | **Traffic** | 2.4 million TEU |
| **Employees** | 1,800 | **Turnover** | €450 M |
| **Governance** | Landlord port | **Main cargo** | Containers, ro-ro, LNG |
| **Culture** | Aware |  |  |

### Assessment

Nord-Maris was built on land reclaimed from the sea. Most terminals sit 4 to 5 metres above mean sea level, behind a dike line designed in the 1960s and raised by 40 cm in 1998. In the winter storm of 2013 the water came within 10 cm of the crest of the western dike. The harbour master still has the photo on his office wall.

Winter storms coming in from the North Sea decide how the port runs between October and March. Above 70 km/h of wind the gantry cranes stop. In January 2024 they stood still for three days in a row and 14 ships waited at anchor. Two shipping lines have since asked for a contingency plan before renewing their contracts.

The logistics zone was added later, on lower ground next to the canal that drains the surrounding polders. After a week of rain in 2021 the canal overflowed onto two truck parks for six hours. The same winter, a cold spell froze rail switches for four days.

Summers are mild, although the reefer stacks needed extra cooling on a few hot days in 2025. Fog is still the most frequent cause of pilotage delays, and the pilots say it has always been that way. The insurers want an updated flood risk assessment before the next renewal in 2027.

### Already in place

- Dike line from the 1960s, raised by 40 cm in 1998

- Wind sensors on the cranes, but no storm pins or tie-downs

- Monitoring pilot on the main breakwater

- No flood plan for the substations; one diesel generator for the control tower

## Port 2 · Meridia

**CLIMATE VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT · 2026 · prepared for the Port of Meridia**

| **Location** | Northern Mediterranean coast | **Climate zone** | Mediterranean |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coastal setting** | Bay or lagoon | **Port type** | Multipurpose & cruise port |
| **Size** | Medium | **Traffic** | 3.1 Mt cargo + 600,000 cruise passengers |
| **Employees** | 380 | **Turnover** | €75 M |
| **Governance** | Public port | **Main cargo** | General cargo, containers, cruise |
| **Culture** | Reactive |  |  |

### Assessment

Meridia sits at the back of a deep, calm bay. The sea rarely causes trouble; the land around it does more and more. The container yard is black asphalt with almost no shade, and in July its surface goes above 60 °C by early afternoon. Straddle carriers overheat, reefer plugs trip, and the occupational health service recorded 37 heat-related incidents last summer.

The pine hills behind the port burned in 2017 and again in 2022. Both times smoke and police roadblocks closed the only motorway access for more than a day, and trucks queued back into town. On a few winter days a year a cold northerly wind comes down the valley: in 2023 it pushed a cruise ship hard against its fenders, and the pilots now close the cruise quay when gusts pass 60 km/h.

In autumn the dry torrent that crosses the old town can fill within hours. In 2019 it overflowed after 180 mm of rain in six hours and the water reached the passenger terminal. The port takes its water from the regional network, which restricted industrial users in two of the last five summers.

The city has launched a 'resilient coast' programme and is looking for a flagship partner. Cruise lines, which bring a third of the port's revenue, have started asking about shore-side heat plans for passengers.

### Already in place

- Nothing formal

- One shaded rest area near the main gate

- The regional fire plan for the hills does not include the port

- Sandbags for the passenger terminal, stored in the basement that flooded in 2019

## Port 3 · Riu Verd

**CLIMATE VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT · 2026 · prepared for the Port of Riu Verd**

| **Location** | River port, Southern Europe, 80 km from the sea | **Climate zone** | Mediterranean (inland) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coastal setting** | Estuary or river | **Port type** | Inland river port with sea lock |
| **Size** | Medium | **Traffic** | 4.5 Mt (grain, fertiliser, containers) |
| **Employees** | 450 | **Turnover** | €90 M |
| **Governance** | Landlord port | **Main cargo** | Agri-bulk, fertiliser, containers |
| **Culture** | Aware |  |  |

### Assessment

Riu Verd lies 80 km upriver, reached through a dredged channel and a single sea lock. Everything depends on how much water the river carries, and the river has become unreliable in both directions. In the winter of 2019 it rose into the grain terminal and closed operations for eleven days. The marks are still on the warehouse walls.

Five summers later the same channel was so shallow that bulk carriers had to leave 30% of their cargo behind. Upstream reservoirs are shared with farmers and cities, and the river basin authority now negotiates releases week by week. The channel silts up faster after every flood, and the dredging campaign was postponed in 2022 and again in 2024 for lack of budget.

Summers are long. Stevedores start at 6 a.m. and stop before 2 p.m. in July and August, an arrangement that was never written down. In 2023 a grass fire along the rail corridor stopped trains for an afternoon.

Two of the three main grain traders have started sending part of their volumes by truck to a sea port 90 km away when the river is low. The port's board sees this as a commercial problem; the harbour master sees it as a climate one.

### Already in place

- Sea lock rebuilt in 1990, inspected by hand twice a year

- Annual dredging campaign (postponed in 2022 and 2024)

- Sandbags and two mobile pumps bought after the 2019 flood

- Early summer shifts, agreed informally

## Port 4 · Boreal

**CLIMATE VULNERABILITY ASSESSMENT · 2026 · prepared for the Port of Boreal**

| **Location** | Northern Baltic coast | **Climate zone** | Cold / sub-arctic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Coastal setting** | Open coast | **Port type** | Ferry, fishing & timber port |
| **Size** | Small | **Traffic** | 0.6 Mt + 400,000 ferry passengers |
| **Employees** | 110 | **Turnover** | €22 M |
| **Governance** | Public port (municipal) | **Main cargo** | Timber, fish, ro-pax |
| **Culture** | Aware |  |  |

### Assessment

Boreal has worked this stretch of Baltic coast for four centuries, and its people talk about weather the way farmers talk about soil. Winter sets the rhythm. Sea ice forms in the inner harbour most years, freezing rain coats cranes and gangways, and the road inland becomes impassable several times a season. In February 2024, eleven days below −20 °C froze the hydraulics of both cranes and cost three weeks of timber exports.

Autumn brings the gales. The shallow Baltic builds steep waves quickly, westerly winds pin the ferry against its berth, and when the wind blows for days from the same direction the water piles up in the bay. The 2023 surge reached +1.9 m with almost no tide.

The road to the motorway belongs to the municipality, which clears it after the town centre. Some winters the timber trucks wait half a day. The ferry company has asked twice for the berth to be kept free of ice; both times the answer was that the port has no budget for it.

Summers are short but getting warmer, and the ferry now runs air conditioning on its crossings for the first time. Boreal has local knowledge and a crew that improvises well. What it lacks is anything written down.

### Already in place

- Reinforced mooring at the ferry berth (2016)

- Two snow ploughs, both over 15 years old

- No written procedures; the harbour master's phone list

- No ice management at the berths

Sabkha was dredged out of a salt flat twenty years ago and has become one of the region's busiest transhipment hubs. Its business is speed: berth productivity is the figure the board looks at every week.

# 4. The seven extreme weather events

Each event has one short name and a code used on the cards and the wheel. Storms, floods, wind and heat are the hazards ports report most often in the UNCTAD survey. Drought and wildfire are growing risks for southern European ports (SAFARI D3.10), and snow and ice are part of SAFARI's own risk list (D3.13).

| **Code** | **Event** | **What happens** | **Parts of the port hit** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **SS** | Storm surge | Low pressure and strong onshore winds push the sea above its normal level and over quays and dikes. | Quays, container yards, substations, warehouses, ground-level equipment |
| **RF** | River flooding | Long or intense rain makes rivers, canals and torrents overflow onto port land. | Rail yards, low-lying storage, access roads, basements, lock gates |
| **HW** | Heatwave | Several days of very high temperatures, often with warm nights and high humidity. | Outdoor workers, crane and vehicle cooling, reefers, IT, power demand, rail tracks |
| **WS** | Windstorm | Sustained high winds and violent gusts, from winter storms, tropical cyclones or local winds. | Cranes, moored ships, container stacks, roofs, overhead lines, loose cargo |
| **DR** | Drought | A long dry spell lowers rivers and puts pressure on the water supply. | Navigation channels, locks, barge traffic, draught limits, water-dependent operations |
| **WF** | Wildfire | Fire in the vegetation around the port or along its access roads and railways. | Access roads and rail, power lines, workers (smoke), fuel and chemical storage |
| **SI** | Snow & ice | Heavy snow, freezing rain or days of deep frost that stop surface operations. | Cranes (ice), rail switches, road access, pipes and hydraulics, worker safety |

Sea-level rise and erosion are slow changes, not events on the wheel. They show up in the game through the rising likelihood of storm surge between 2026 and 2036.

## Likelihood and potential loss are two different things

The wheel only decides how likely each event is. How much an event would cost if it hit, unprotected, is a separate number — the potential loss. Each event carries one potential loss, in € millions, the same for every port: a storm surge is the biggest single event anywhere, a heatwave the smallest (section 7). What differs between ports is how *likely* each event is (the wheel) and which solutions make physical sense there (the deck). A card that is excellent against surge is wasted where the wheel almost never draws surge.

## The events worksheet

Before choosing any card, each team ranks the seven events by how much they should worry about them — combining how likely the event is for their port with how much it would cost — and writes one line on why. This forces the team to read the report and the 'Already in place' section and to agree on what matters most. The potential losses are printed on the event cards, so this is a prioritisation exercise, not a guessing game.

## Card texts

**1. Coastal defence structures****   ●●●●● 5 coins**

Seawalls, breakwaters and revetments at the edge of the port. They take the energy out of waves and keep most storm surges out, up to the storm they were designed for. A bigger storm can still go over the top. Expensive and long-lasting, and no help against hazards that come from the land side.

*Protection: SS 8 · WS 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal, Coralia*

**2. Elevated critical infrastructure****   ●●●● 4 coins**

Substations, control rooms, pumps and IT are moved or raised above the expected flood level, and new berths are built higher. When the water comes in, the equipment that keeps the port running stays dry. Very effective against storm surge, useful against river floods, but it only protects what has been raised.

*Protection: SS 7 · RF 4   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Coralia, Sabkha*

**3. Deployable flood barriers****   ●●● 3 coins**

Temporary barriers, including modular 3D-printed units, put across gates, doors and low points when a flood is forecast. Much cheaper than a permanent wall, and they work against both sea and river water. They only help if the warning comes in time and the team knows where to install them.

*Protection: SS 5 · RF 5   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Riu Verd, Coralia, Sabkha*

**4. Green infrastructure ****&**** permeable surfaces****   ●● 2 coins**

Green roofs, rain gardens and permeable paving let water soak into the ground instead of running straight into drains and channels. They reduce flooding up to the volume they were designed for, and planted areas lower temperatures a little on hot days. A very large flood will still overwhelm them.

*Protection: RF 5 · HW 2   ·   Ports: Meridia*

**5. Nature-based coastal ****&**** riverbank protection****   ●● 2 coins**

Restored wetlands, mangroves, salt marsh and planted riverbanks, sometimes built with dredged sediment. They hold the shoreline in place, slow incoming water and bring wildlife back. They protect less than concrete and take years to grow, but they get stronger over time rather than weaker.

*Protection: SS 4 · RF 3 · WS 2   ·   Ports: Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**6. Structural health monitoring****   ●● 2 coins**

Sensors on locks, quay walls, cranes and rail track that measure stress, vibration and movement day and night. Monitoring stops no storm. It shows which structure is close to failing, so it can be repaired before a flood, gale, heatwave or hard frost breaks it.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 2 · WS 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal*

**7. Digital twin ****&**** predictive maintenance****   ●●●● 4 coins**

A virtual copy of a critical asset, such as a lock or a rail network, fed with live data. It predicts how the asset wears down under heat, frost, floods or low water, and plans maintenance before it fails. Most useful in river ports, where it helps run the lock when water levels get critical.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 2 · WS 2 · DR 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Riu Verd*

**8. Early warning ****&**** forecasting system****   ●● 2 coins**

Weather alerts made for the port, with thresholds for wind, rain, heat, frost and water levels that trigger action. A warning stops nothing on its own, but a few hours of notice let the port secure cargo, stop cranes, move equipment and protect people. It helps a little against every hazard.

*Protection: SS 2 · RF 2 · HW 2 · WS 2 · DR 2 · WF 2 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**9. Real-time operations platform****   ●●●● 4 coins**

A shared digital platform that reroutes ships, trains and trucks before and during a disruption, sees congestion coming and organises the restart. It doesn't protect any asset. It keeps cargo moving while the event lasts and cuts the operational losses of floods, storms, low water and ice.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · WS 3 · DR 3 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Riu Verd*

**10. Emergency response plan ****&**** drills****   ●● 2 coins**

A plan that sets out who does what, evacuation routes, who calls whom and how to protect equipment for each hazard, tested with regular drills. People are protected first, then assets. It reduces losses in almost every kind of event, provided it has actually been practised.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 3 · WS 3 · WF 2 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**11. Worker heat-protection protocol****   ● 1 coin**

Shaded rest areas, water points, earlier shifts and heat-stress monitoring for outdoor staff. In a port, much of what a heatwave costs comes from workers falling ill and hours lost, and this cheap measure deals with exactly that. It also helps when wildfire smoke makes outdoor work unsafe.

*Protection: HW 7 · WF 2   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd, Sabkha*

**12. Climate-resilient materials ****&**** retrofits****   ●●●● 4 coins**

Buildings, warehouses and workshops are reinforced with materials designed for strong winds, driving rain, extreme heat and repeated frost. Some protection against almost everything, most of all against wind. It covers buildings, not yards, channels or cranes, and it is expensive.

*Protection: SS 3 · RF 3 · HW 3 · WS 5 · WF 2 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Meridia, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**13. Wind-resistant mooring ****&**** crane reinforcement****   ●●● 3 coins**

Stronger mooring systems, crane tie-downs, storm pins, automatic stops linked to wind speed and secured container stacks. Cranes and moored ships are where wind does the most damage in a port, and this measure targets them directly. Very effective in gales and cyclones, of little use against ice, none against water.

*Protection: WS 7 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal, Coralia*

**14. Backup power ****&**** microgrid****   ●●● 3 coins**

Generators, batteries and solar panels on site keep pumps, cooling, reefers, communications and IT running when the grid goes down. A power cut makes every other problem worse. Most useful in heatwaves, when demand peaks, and in ice storms, when lines come down; little use once water is inside the building.

*Protection: SS 1 · RF 1 · HW 4 · WS 2 · WF 1 · SI 3   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**15. Drainage system upgrade****   ●●● 3 coins**

Bigger stormwater pipes, new pumping stations and cleared channels, sized for today's rainfall and river levels rather than those of 1980. The most direct way to get flood water off the port quickly. A flood beyond the new design can still overwhelm it, and it does nothing against the sea.

*Protection: RF 7   ·   Ports: Meridia, Coralia*

**16. Alternative water supply ****&**** reuse****   ●●● 3 coins**

Rainwater harvesting, reuse of treated wastewater and a second, independent supply such as a small desalination unit. When drought restricts the main supply, the port can still wash, cool, keep dust down and fight fires. Essential where water is scarce, and it adds some cooling capacity in heatwaves.

*Protection: HW 1 · DR 6 · WF 2   ·   Ports: Meridia, Sabkha*

**17. Multimodal shift platform****   ●● 2 coins**

Rail sidings, barge connections and agreements that let cargo switch between road, rail and water when one route is cut: flooded roads, closed terminals, low rivers, icy motorways. It protects the flow of goods rather than the assets.

*Protection: SS 2 · RF 3 · WS 3 · DR 2 · WF 2 · SI 2   ·   Ports: Riu Verd, Boreal*

**18. Artificial reefs ****&**** wave dissipation****   ●●● 3 coins**

Underwater structures, including 3D-printed reefs made from natural materials, that break waves before they reach the port's defences and create new habitat. They reduce the force of storm waves and slow erosion. A surge still raises the water level, so they reduce the damage without stopping it.

*Protection: SS 4 · WS 2   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Coralia*

**19. Governance ****&**** institutional protocols****   ● 1 coin**

Written rules on who decides what, when and with whom, before and during extreme weather, agreed between the port authority, terminal operators, the city and emergency services. On its own it protects nothing. When decisions are slow or unclear, even good technical measures fail; with clear rules every other card works better.

*Protection: Raises the culture level by one   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**20. Capacity building ****&**** training programme****   ● 1 coin**

Regular training for staff on each hazard: e-learning, scenario exercises, simulations across departments. Trained people act faster and improvise less. Like governance, it protects nothing on its own; it makes the plan, the warnings and the equipment work when they are needed.

*Protection: Raises the culture level by one   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**21. Adaptive dredging ****&**** sediment management****   ●●● 3 coins**

Drone and sonar surveys show where the channel silts up, dredging is targeted, and the sediment is reused to strengthen the banks. For river and estuary ports it is the first answer to low-water draught limits, and a clear channel also carries flood water away faster.

*Protection: RF 3 · DR 6   ·   Ports: Riu Verd*

**22. Stormwater retention ****&**** storage basins****   ●●● 3 coins**

Basins and tanks that catch peak flows before they flood the port, then store the water for dry periods: dust control, cooling, firefighting. One installation for two opposite problems, too much water and too little.

*Protection: RF 6 · DR 3 · WF 1   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd*

**23. Parametric climate insurance****   ● 1 coin**

Insurance that pays out automatically when a measured threshold is crossed (wind speed, water level, temperature), without a long loss assessment. It prevents no damage, but it softens the financial blow and pays for a quick recovery. Moving the risk to an insurer doesn't make the port safer.

*Protection: SS 1 · RF 1 · HW 1 · WS 1 · DR 1 · WF 1 · SI 1   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Meridia, Riu Verd, Boreal, Coralia, Sabkha*

**24. Winterisation package****   ●●● 3 coins**

De-icing systems for cranes and gantries, heated rail switches, insulated pipes and hydraulics, and cold-weather gear for staff. During an ice storm it decides whether the cranes work or stand still. It does nothing for the rest of the year.

*Protection: SI 7   ·   Ports: Boreal*

**25. Firebreaks ****&**** vegetation management****   ●● 2 coins**

Cleared buffer strips, managed vegetation along the fence line, roads and railways, and joint plans with the regional fire service. Wildfire reaches a port through its surroundings: access roads, power lines, the edge of fuel storage. Managing that strip keeps the fire, and most of the disruption, further away.

*Protection: HW 1 · WF 6   ·   Ports: Meridia, Riu Verd*

**26. Cool surfaces ****&**** shaded yards****   ● 1 coin**

Reflective coatings on yards and roofs, shade structures over work areas and reefer stacks, and light-coloured paving. Surfaces stay several degrees cooler, equipment overheats less and outdoor work can go on for longer. It helps only against heat.

*Protection: HW 5   ·   Ports: Meridia, Sabkha*

**27. Snow ****&**** ice clearance fleet****   ●● 2 coins**

Modern snow ploughs, gritting stock, de-icing for yards and access roads, and an agreement with the municipality so the port road is cleared first. Trucks keep moving and staff can get to work. It doesn't help frozen cranes or iced-up berths.

*Protection: SI 4   ·   Ports: Nord-Maris, Boreal*

**28. Harbour ice management****   ●●● 3 coins**

Air-bubbler systems that keep berths free of ice, plus a shared contract for an ice-class tug. Ferries and cargo ships can still dock when the harbour freezes over. Only useful where sea ice forms.

*Protection: SI 5   ·   Ports: Boreal*

# 6. Scoring

The app does all the calculations. This section is for facilitators who need to answer questions.

## Protection against the event drawn

The app takes every card the team owns that scores on the event, applies the culture multiplier c, and treats the cards as layers: each one stops part of what got through the others.

**Protection P = 10 × [ 1 − (1 − c·s₁/10) × (1 − c·s₂/10) × … ]**

P never goes above 10. Two cards of 5 give 7.5, not 10, and one well-chosen card is worth more than several weak ones.

## The numbers after each spin

Display units only; the formulas are unchanged. All money is shown in € millions, whole numbers. Coins stay on screen only for the budget and the card costs — they are never money. Each event has one potential loss, the same for every port and round (section 7).

| **Number** | **How it is calculated** |
| --- | --- |
| Protection this event (%) | P × 10, for the event the wheel drew (P from the formula above). |
| Damage sustained (€M) | Potential loss of the drawn event × (1 − P/10), shown in € millions. |
| Potential losses (€M) | The drawn event's potential loss — what it would cost with none of the team's solutions. The same figure for every port. |
| Losses avoided (€M) | Potential losses − damage sustained. |
| Your strategy (/10) | (Expected protection ÷ the best expected protection the port could reach with the same budget and starting culture level) × 10. The app finds the best by testing every affordable combination. Expected protection — damage avoided across all events, each weighted by its likelihood on the wheel and its potential loss, divided by the total possible damage; it does not depend on what the wheel drew — is still computed for the score but no longer shown on its own. |

## Rounds and time

- Round 1, 2026: 3 coins. The wheel is unknown when teams choose; they rely on the report and their own reading of what would hurt most.

- At the first spin the app shows the 2026 wheel.

- Round 2, 2031: +3 coins (6 cumulative). Unspent coins carry over. A team may return any number of its cards before the spin, each for its full cost, and spend the coins on others. The wheel shifts to 2031.

- Round 3, 2036: +3 coins (9 cumulative). A team may again return any number of its cards before the spin, each for its full cost, and spend the coins on others. The wheel shifts again (the potential losses do not change — only the likelihoods do).

- Final ranking: the average strategy score over the three rounds. The total damage over the three spins is shown next to it as the 'luck' result.

Why both numbers: in a test of 20,000 simulated games run on the v4 rules, a median strategy did as well as or better than the best one in 20–30% of single spins at four of the six ports, and still up to 17% of the time over three spins. Ranking on damage alone would reward luck.

## Worked example: Nord-Maris in 2026

Nord-Maris starts Developing (× 1.0). Its 2026 wheel: storm surge 40%, windstorm 30%, river flooding 10%, heatwave 10%, snow & ice 10%. Potential losses are the same for every port: storm surge €600M, windstorm €500M, river flooding €400M, snow & ice €300M, heatwave €200M.

With 6 coins the team buys Elevated critical infrastructure (#2, 4 coins), Governance & institutional protocols (#19, 1 coin, an enabler that lifts the culture to Proactive, × 1.1) and Parametric climate insurance (#23, 1 coin). #2 covers storm surge and river flooding well; the enabler and the insurance add a thin layer against everything. The wheel draws snow & ice, which #2 does not touch, so only the culture lift and the insurance apply: P = 1.1, protection 11%, and the damage is €300M × (1 − 0.11) = €267M instead of €300M — €33M avoided.

| **Event** | **Likelihood** | **Potential loss** | **Protection** | **Damage avoided (likelihood × loss × P/10)** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Storm surge | 40% | €600M | 80% | 190.9 |
| Windstorm | 30% | €500M | 11% | 16.5 |
| River flooding | 10% | €400M | 50% | 20.1 |
| Heatwave | 10% | €200M | 11% | 2.2 |
| Snow & ice | 10% | €300M | 11% | 3.3 |
| Total |  | possible: 480 | | avoided: 232.9 |

Expected protection: 232.9 ÷ 480 = 48.5%. The best possible for Nord-Maris with 6 coins, starting Developing, is 60.6%. Strategy score: 8.0 / 10. The plan is well judged — it covers the two events that carry most of the likely damage (surge and flooding) — yet the wheel drew the one hazard it left open. A good expected plan can still meet bad luck on the day, which is why the final ranking averages three rounds.

## Messages to take home

- Read the risk before buying the solution: the same card can be excellent in one port and almost useless in another.

- How likely an event is and how much it would cost are two separate questions, and both matter.

- Start with measures that pay off in every scenario, then add heavy infrastructure as budget and evidence allow.

- The climate of 2036 is not the climate of 2026. A plan has to be revisited.

- Being well prepared lowers the odds of a bad outcome. It does not remove them.

# 9. How the numbers were built, and their limits

Protection scores follow a simple rule. Engineered measures designed against one hazard score 7 or 8, never 9 or 10, because design standards get exceeded by extreme events. Physical measures that cover only part of a hazard or part of the port score 4 to 6. Warning and operational measures score 2 or 3. Early warning is anchored on the estimate that 24 hours of warning can cut damage by about 30%, scored at 2 because a port can act on only part of a warning.

The game balance was tested by computing every affordable combination of cards for every port, round and culture level. With one potential loss per event and a 3-coin opening budget, the best expected protection in 2026 (Aware) ranges from about 31% at Sabkha to 35% at Meridia across the six ports, and the best combination differs from port to port. Protection then grows across the rounds as the budget builds to 6 and 9 coins. The spread is driven by how each port's wheel weights the larger-loss events.

**Limits to say out loud at the debrief. **Scores, costs, potential losses and wheel weights are teaching estimates, not published values; the literature gives no clean percentages per measure and hazard. The trends between 2026 and 2036 follow the direction of the IPCC findings, not their magnitude. The ports and their data are fictional. The Coralia chart shows stronger storms, not more frequent ones, in line with the IPCC finding that the share of intense tropical cyclones has likely increased.
